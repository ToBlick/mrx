# The boundary-penalty scale `s` — settled

`alpha_k = <m_k sqrt(g^rr)> / <m_k/J> / h_last`, scaled by `s`
(`PRODUCTION_BC_SCALE`). This closes open item 1 of
`preconditioner_lessons.md`.

All numbers: real solve (`apply_inverse_hodge_laplacian` -> saddle MINRES),
`schur.outer = block`, `12,24,12`, p=3, tol 1e-10, maxiter 10000, **free rows
only** — `s` does not enter the Dirichlet rows.

`!` = hit maxiter. No row's optimum sits on a grid edge. **The `s` question is
closed as a measurement** — the answer is no longer waiting on a wider grid.

**Larger `s` is not the answer.** Every row turns around by `s = 32` at the
latest, and `s >= 64` pushes cells that had converged past 10000. The earlier
reading that some geometries "want 16–32" was the top of a *flat* basin, not a
truncated one.

## 2. The flat basin is why a single constant is fine

The per-geometry optima still span 0.5 (hegna) to 16 (quasr9983) — a factor 32,
and that spread is real. But the spread in `argmin` is not the quantity that
matters. Geomean excess over each row's own optimum, by geometry:

Worst geometry, as a function of the single shipped constant:

| `s` | 2 | 2.83 | **3** | **4** | 5.66 | 8 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| max over geometries | 1.36x | 1.28x | **1.27x** | **1.21x** | 1.25x | 1.38x |

**A factor-32 spread in the optimum costs at most 27% in iterations.** `s = 4`
is the minimax point at 1.21x; the shipped `s = 3` gives 1.27x. That is a 5%
difference between them — inside the noise of a single job, and not worth a
default change.

This retires the per-geometry calibration idea. `alpha_exact/alpha_5` predicted
`argmin` at 1.85x spread against a 9.55x null, but `argmin` was never worth
predicting: acting on a perfect prediction buys 27% at most, needs
`alpha_exact` back in the code, and would have to be re-fit per `k` (the
per-geometry curves above already differ by `k`). **Ship the constant.**

## 3. The two ranking criteria, and where they disagree

13 of 18 rows converge at every `s` on the full 0…512 grid. On those:

```

    8     1.046x   1.19x |    37768        1
    16    1.069x   1.39x |    42351        1
    32    1.180x   1.78x |    50971        1
    64    1.392x   2.29x |    58087        3
   512    2.937x   5.08x |    86333        5
```

`capped18` = all 18 rows with a stall charged at 10000.

- **Speed** (13 clean rows) points at `s = 5.7–8`, geomean 1.05x.
- **Robustness** (`capped18`, all 18) points at `s = 2`, and `s in [0.25, 2]`
  is the only band with zero stalls.
- They disagree by ~4x in `s` but the disagreement costs little: `capped18` is
  flat at 35.3k–35.7k across the whole of `[1, 4]`.

The single stall that appears at `s >= 2.83` is **hegna k=2 free**, which runs
9068–10000 across the *entire* grid — it is within 10% of the maxiter budget
even at its own optimum. It is a hegna problem, not an `s` problem, and it
should not be allowed to drive the constant. Charging it as a stall is what
makes the robustness curve prefer 2 over 4.

`s = 0` is the worst value in 17 of 18 rows (2.16x geomean). The term always
earns its place.

## 4. Verdict

**Keep `PRODUCTION_BC_SCALE = 3.0`.** It is within 10% of the speed optimum,
inside the flat part of the robustness curve, and 5% off the minimax point.
The value is now measured rather than assumed, on the real solve path, with the
grid bracketing on both sides on all six geometries.

For the paper: report the basin, not the argmin. The claim is "the method is
insensitive to `s` over roughly `[2, 8]`, and a single constant costs at most
27% against per-geometry tuning" — which is a stronger statement than a tuned
table would have been.

## 5. Still open

1. **hegna k=2 free** does not converge at any `s`. Fourth independent
   measurement flagging this geometry (see `preconditioner_lessons.md` §8.2).
   Not an `s` question.
2. **`h` and `p` dependence of the basin** (sweep plan S4) — everything above is
   `12,24,12`, p=3. `alpha` carries an explicit `1/h_last`, so `s` should be
   `h`-independent by construction; that has not been measured on the real
   solve. This is the last thing that could invalidate a single constant.
